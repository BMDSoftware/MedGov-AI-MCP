A spleen volume exceeding 500 cm³ is clinically significant as it indicates **splenomegaly** (an enlarged spleen). The normal volume of a spleen in an adult is typically between 150 cm³ and 250 cm³.

A volume above 500 cm³ is considered moderate to severe splenomegaly and warrants investigation into the underlying cause, which may include:

*   **Infections:** Such as mononucleosis, malaria, or endocarditis.
*   **Hematologic Disorders:** Including various leukemias, lymphomas, or myeloproliferative neoplasms.
*   **Liver Disease:** Often associated with portal hypertension (e.g., cirrhosis).
*   **Storage Diseases:** Such as Gaucher disease.
*   **Autoimmune Conditions:** Including rheumatoid arthritis or systemic lupus erythematosus.
